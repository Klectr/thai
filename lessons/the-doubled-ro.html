The Doubled รร

รร looks like two r's waiting to be pronounced, and I like that it is doing something else entirely. It is a vowel in disguise, and neither letter gets an r sound.

Which vowel it becomes depends on one quick look ahead. If another consonant follows it inside the same syllable, รร is just the short a you already know from กัน, and that following consonant is the final. So ธรรม reads tham, with a mid tone, and กรรม reads gam the same way. You will meet those two inside bigger everyday words, too: ธรรมดา for ordinary, ธรรมชาติ for nature, and กิจกรรม for activity on school and community notices.

If nothing follows it in its syllable, รร gives you a plus an implied n, the sound of -ัน. That is the first syllable of บรรทุก, read ban, before ทุก takes over, so รถบรรทุก on the road is simply the truck word built from it. In both cases the syllable ends in a sonorant sound, m or n, so it stays live and the short a does not pull the tone down into dead-syllable territory.

For tomorrow morning I would keep it to those three words and ask the position question out loud before you sound anything: is there a final after รร, or is รร closing the syllable itself? Answer that first and the reading follows. I will watch whether that check sticks in your morning practice.
